Introducing
Belfast Duo: Chalk

Emerging from a renowned film program in Belfast, Chalk has pushed new boundaries in the alt-rock world with their debut album, Crystalpunk. The band’s driving duo, Ben Goddard and Ross Cullen, continue to bring their music to life on an ongoing U.S. tour, with plans to take the stage at some of the world’s largest festivals, including Lollapalooza this summer. Recently, our very own Troy Tran had the opportunity to catch up with Chalk at FREQUENCY Fest, a two-day music festival in Dallas, where they explored the origins of the band’s defining ideas and what they hope listeners take away from their immersive sound. From their raw live performance to their cinematic vision, it’s clear that Chalk has a bright future ahead and is steadily growing into a global community for industrial rock listeners everywhere.

TT:  What was the first song you guys bonded over together in person?

RC: Yeah, I would say it would be a Gilla Band song. Maybe “Pears for Lunch” by Gilla Band. 
BG: They’re like an Irish noisy industrial band. “Pears for Lunch.” Check them out, they’re pretty good.
TT: (Directed toward Ross) What Gilla Band poster did Ben see on your Instagram, and where did you get it?
RC: I got that poster on sale at Gilla Bands' website. Ever since then, I got it in a nice frame. I put it on my Instagram one time, and the band started because of that.
BG:  Was it like a European-run poster or something like that? German dates or something?
RC: Yeah. I didn’t go to see them live. I just bought the poster on the website because it was signed and on sale.  
TT: What was the first project that felt like chalk?
RC: “Them?”
BG: Yeah, probably the first song we ever did. Well, we'd wrote music as a different band before, but this one we kinda put a lot into it. We paid for, like, a radio plug-in and a music video, and we’d kind of brainstormed this whole image of a band behind one song, and then from that, that's all we kind of all had for a couple of months, and then we decided we needed more songs.
TT: How does it feel playing SXSW vs playing back at home (Ireland)?
BG: Showcase festivals are always funny because the first day we played a show at 4 p.m. outside in 36° C (97° F) heat, and we almost died of sunstroke. And then we played another show at like 11 p.m. in a like very dark, quite a nice room, but still a very dark room. So, SXSW is always crazy, and it’s got a whole other manic energy, but playing back home is always our favorite place to play. We always think about playing back home when we’re writing songs on stage to play them. Nothing can beat playing in Belfast.

RC: Well, there's a lot of history in the country and with dance music in the 90s and probably in the 70s and 80s, so growing up, if your parents were into that sort of stuff, they would have shared that history, and also around the city to have managed the history of music affairs and the music center. BG: It's an interesting question. I mean, definitely visuals big time, it just kind of gave us a place where we could kind of figure out who we were and what we want to say.
